For a first trip to Bangkok, use a foreign card at hotels, malls and larger restaurants, and carry baht for street stalls, markets and local transport. For transit, use Rabbit on the BTS, contactless bank cards on the MRT Blue and Purple lines, and a bank card on the Airport Rail Link; keep cash for the places and journeys where cards may not work.

## Where can I pay by card?

Foreign Visa, Mastercard and UnionPay cards work at hotels, malls and larger restaurants. Carry a backup payment method, since street stalls and markets generally need cash. Tuk-tuks and most meter taxis also need cash, though ride-hailing apps can charge a registered foreign card.

Confirm Amex acceptance before relying on it. At smaller merchants, ask whether a card surcharge applies before paying. If a terminal offers to convert the amount into your home currency, choose THB instead.

For more trip-planning guidance, see Localley’s [Thailand payment guide](/help/thailand/pay).

## How much cash should I carry?

Carry baht for street food, markets, local transport and taxi tolls. A suggested daily reserve for street meals and modest market purchases is THB500–1,000. Keep THB20, THB50 and THB100 notes handy: drivers may struggle to change THB1,000.

You do not need to rely on large notes for small purchases. Before leaving your hotel or a larger shop, check that you have smaller notes available for fares and everyday spending.

## Which payment works on Bangkok transit?

For the BTS, buy a Rabbit card at a station office using your passport, then reload it with cash. Rabbit requires a physical card, and it does not cover the MRT Blue or Purple lines. Its purchase fee is non-refundable; ask a BTS office about a refund for eligible remaining value.

On the MRT Blue and Purple lines, you can tap a contactless Visa, Mastercard or UnionPay card. The Airport Rail Link added bank-card payments in November 2025. Keep baht available for other local transport and for journeys where your payment method is not accepted. As of 2026-10, these transit rules and payment options are current.

## Where should I withdraw cash, and what should I avoid?

Krungsri and SCB ATMs accept foreign cards. Use machines at banks or malls, cover the keypad while entering your PIN, and decline conversion to your home currency. Your card issuer may add its own fee.

As of 2026-10, Krungsri charges THB350 for Mastercard withdrawals; Visa and UnionPay withdrawals cost THB250. Check the ATM screen before confirming, and remember that an issuer may charge an additional fee.

## Can I pay by QR code?

TAGTHAi Easy Pay offers tourists QR payments through KBank PAY&TOUR. Register with your passport at a participating exchange booth. To fund PAY&TOUR, exchange cash at KBank and then link it in TAGTHAi. Do not assume that a foreign card can top it up.

Some supported home banking apps can pay participating Thai merchants through cross-border QR links. This depends on the app and merchant: ordinary personal PromptPay codes may fail. Keep baht or another accepted payment method ready if the QR payment is not available.

## Should I tip?

Tipping is optional. Rounding a taxi fare is common, and at restaurants you can check the bill for an included service charge before deciding whether to leave anything extra.

## FAQ

### Can I use a foreign card for every purchase?

No. Cards work at hotels, malls and larger restaurants, but street stalls, markets, tuk-tuks and most meter taxis need cash. Ride-hailing apps can charge a registered foreign card.

### Can I use Rabbit on the MRT?

No. Rabbit is for the BTS and excludes the MRT Blue and Purple lines. Those MRT lines accept contactless Visa, Mastercard and UnionPay.

### Should I accept an ATM’s conversion to my home currency?

No. Decline that conversion and use THB. Your issuer may still charge an additional fee.

### Can I add money to PAY&TOUR with my foreign card?

Do not assume so. Fund PAY&TOUR with cash exchanged at KBank, then link it in TAGTHAi.

### How can I plan payments around my itinerary?

Match your payment options to the places and transit you expect to use, and keep a cash reserve for street spending. You can [start a Bangkok itinerary](/itineraries/new?city=Bangkok) and plan where to carry cash or use a card.
