In Tokyo, pay by foreign Visa or Mastercard at many hotels, chains and larger restaurants, and use yen cash for small businesses and places that may not take cards. For getting around, choose a physical Welcome Suica or, if you have a compatible iPhone or Apple Watch, Welcome Suica Mobile; withdraw yen from a Seven Bank or Japan Post ATM when needed.

## Where can I pay by card?

Foreign Visa and Mastercard work widely at hotels, chains and larger restaurants. Amex also works at many larger businesses. When choosing where to eat or stay, these cards are a practical way to pay at larger businesses, but don’t assume every shop or restaurant accepts them.

Check UnionPay acceptance before ordering. Ask before you place your order so you can choose another payment method if needed. Many urban taxis accept cards, but a small restaurant or a business in a rural area may require cash. Keep yen available for those situations rather than relying on a card for every payment.

As of 2026-10, the general PIN bypass for card payments ended in March 2025. Know your card PIN before you travel. If a payment terminal requests it, signing may not replace entering your PIN, so check that you can use your card with a PIN before setting out.

For more payment guidance, see Localley’s [Japan payment tips](/help/japan/pay).

## How much yen cash should I carry?

Carry yen for markets, small restaurants, shrines, lockers and rural transport. These are the kinds of stops where cash may be needed, even if you use a card at larger businesses. Keep JPY1,000 notes and coins with you, so you have smaller amounts available for purchases.

As of 2026-10, a suggested daily cash reserve is JPY5,000–10,000 for casual meals, markets and small purchases. Carry more if you plan to shop. Treat the reserve as a practical starting point: your needs depend on what you plan to do, and the cash should cover the places where you cannot use your preferred card.

Before leaving for a day out, check that you have yen in smaller notes and coins. That way, a cash-only purchase or a stop at a small restaurant does not depend on finding a card terminal.

## Which Welcome Suica should I use?

You can buy a physical Welcome Suica at designated JR East airport machines. Reload that physical card with cash. If you want to use a physical card, remember that reloading it is a cash task; plan to have yen available.

Welcome Suica Mobile is another option for visitors with compatible iPhones and Apple Watches. It launched in March 2025 and supports Apple Pay card reloads. Check whether your device is compatible before choosing this option, and use Apple Pay to reload the mobile card.

As of 2026-10, Welcome Suica balances are non-refundable. Physical cards expire after 28 days, while Welcome Suica Mobile expires after 180 days. Keep those limits in mind when deciding how much to load, and avoid adding more than you expect to use.

## Where can I withdraw yen?

Seven Bank machines inside 7-Eleven accept major foreign networks, including Amex and UnionPay. Japan Post is another reliable option. These are places to consider when you need yen for cash-only purchases or want to replenish the cash you carry.

As of 2026-10, Japan Post charges JPY220, while Seven Bank fees depend on the card. Your card issuer may also charge a fee, so check its fees before withdrawing. At the ATM, choose yen, cover your PIN as you enter it and check the fee details shown before completing the withdrawal.

## Can I use a QR wallet, and should I tip?

If you already use a supported wallet, including Alipay or GCash, you can scan participating PayPay merchant codes with your home wallet. Support applies at participating merchants, so don’t assume every shop displaying a QR code will accept your wallet. Check that your wallet supports payments in Japan before departure.

As of 2026-10, PayPay expanded support to 35 overseas payment services in June 2026. The expanded support does not mean every wallet is supported. Check your home wallet’s Japan support before your trip, and have yen or a card available in case a merchant does not participate.

Tipping is not customary. Pay the bill, including any stated service or table charge. Check the bill for a stated charge and pay it as part of the amount due; there is no need to add a tip.

## FAQ

### Should I rely on my card for every purchase?

No. Cards work widely at hotels, chains and larger restaurants, but small restaurants and rural businesses may require cash. Carry yen for those stops, markets, shrines, lockers and rural transport.

### Can I reload a physical Welcome Suica with a card?

The physical Welcome Suica is reloaded with cash. Welcome Suica Mobile supports Apple Pay card reloads for compatible iPhones and Apple Watches.

### Should I choose my home currency at an ATM?

Choose yen. Cover your PIN while entering it, and check both the ATM fee and your issuer’s fees before you withdraw.

### Should I leave a tip after a meal?

Tipping is not customary. Pay the bill, including any stated service or table charge.

### How can I plan payments around my itinerary?

Match your payment options to the places you plan to visit: carry yen for cash-needed stops, and check card or wallet acceptance before ordering. You can [plan a Tokyo itinerary](/itineraries/new?city=Tokyo) and consider where you may want cash on hand.
