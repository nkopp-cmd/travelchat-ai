Three Easy Days in Chiang Mai is a relaxed three-day plan for first-time visitors who want to explore the city through temples, local food, crafts, and quiet breaks.

Each day has a clear theme and a few stops, with time to look around, browse, or sit down. Costs are listed as planned so you can decide where you want to buy something or order food and drinks.

## Day 1: Teak, Books, and Jazz

![Painted illustration for day 1 in Chiang Mai: Teak, books, and jazz in Si Phum](/media/trip-story/8229a094be8dc9efd08d849041cdd7651d16a4a8b3836169f3f495d7ff5091a6-1080.webp)

Start in Si Phum with a slow look at Wat Phan Tao at 10:00 AM. Allow 30 minutes, with no purchase planned. This teak temple is often overlooked beside famous Wat Chedi Luang, the site of Yi Peng lantern releases. Pause outside to see how the wooden hall fits together, then take your time looking around.

At 2:00 PM, spend 30 minutes at On the Road Books, a bookshop in Chiang Mai’s Old City. Browse the shelves and flip through a few books; pick one up if you want something to read later. A book purchase is optional, and the shop offers a quiet break from sightseeing.

End the day at North Gate Jazz Co-op at 8:30 PM. Set aside an hour to settle in for live jazz at this small venue near the north gate and stay for a set. The intimate Old City venue features local and international musicians; drinks are as ordered.

## Day 2: Forest Paths and Crafts

![Painted illustration for day 2 in Chiang Mai: Forest paths and crafts in Suthep](/media/trip-story/e2b2e4a023e6ff85c552d85523fcb718f9223f656743f2baa8b704c40974b9d3-1080.webp)

Head to Wat Umong at 9:30 AM and allow an hour. There is no purchase planned. Walk the shaded grounds and explore the temple tunnels, looking for small altars in the alcoves. The forest temple has 14th-century tunnels, Buddha altars, and miniature caves, making it a place for quiet contemplation.

At 11:00 AM, spend an hour at Baan Kang Wat (Art Village). Browse the small shops for handmade goods, and take a coffee break if you feel like staying longer. This craft market and artist village outside the old city has quaint shops and cozy cafés. Coffee and shopping are optional.

At 12:30 PM, take 30 minutes to try Sai Ua (Northern Sausage). Look for it at a street stall, try a portion, and taste the herbs. This herb-filled pork sausage is unique to Northern Thailand and is best from street stalls. Food is as ordered.

## Day 3: Market Snacks and Slow Lunch

![Painted illustration for day 3 in Chiang Mai: Market snacks and a slow lunch in Chang Phueak and Pa Tan](/media/trip-story/3df27179687a9dcf0813d74e8a4793d3b1b0b308d2a3c35cac436c7ba75d5d50-1080.webp)

Begin at Siri Wattana Market at 9:00 AM, allowing 45 minutes. Browse the fresh produce and prepared food, then pick up a snack that catches your eye; snacks are as ordered. Also called Thanin Market, it sells fresh ingredients and prepared food in Chang Phueak, where you can browse everyday groceries alongside neighbourhood shoppers.

At 12:00 PM, sit down at Talung Southern Thai Food for an hour-long lunch. Try Southern Thai food in Chiang Mai’s Chang Phueak area, and ask which dish the kitchen recommends if you’re new to the region’s cooking. It’s a chance to explore a different regional cooking tradition while travelling in northern Thailand. Lunch is as ordered.

Finish at Yorice Cafe at 2:30 PM. Allow 45 minutes to choose something from the bakery and have it with a coffee, then leave the rest of the afternoon open. The cafe combines a bakery and health food restaurant in Pa Tan, with coffee or a meal available. Coffee and bakery items are as ordered.
