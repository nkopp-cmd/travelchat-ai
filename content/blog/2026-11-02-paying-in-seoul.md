Seoul is one of the easiest cities in Asia to pay in. A foreign Visa or Mastercard works in most shops, cafés, convenience stores and taxis. A few places still need cash, and the transit card is a separate step. This guide tells you what to carry, where cash still matters and what to say at the counter.

## Cards: your main way to pay

- Foreign **Visa and Mastercard** work widely in shops, restaurants, convenience stores and taxis.
- **Amex and UnionPay** work at many larger merchants. Ask before you order at small restaurants and independent shops.
- Most stores have **no card minimum**, so you can pay for one coffee or one bottle of water by card.
- Carry a **physical card**. Apple Pay and Google Pay with foreign cards work in fewer places than at home.

## Cash: keep a small reserve

You still need cash for traditional markets, street food stalls, small rural businesses and topping up a physical transit card.

- Carry **KRW 50,000–100,000** a day for food and market purchases.
- Ask for small notes: **KRW 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000**. Market stalls often cannot change a KRW 50,000 note.

## Transit: get a T-money card on day one

- Buy a **T-money** card at any convenience store (GS25, CU, 7-Eleven) or at participating subway stations.
- Top-ups on the physical card are **cash only** at most machines. Keep a few KRW 10,000 notes for this.
- Compatible Galaxy phones can use the **Korea Tour Card** app with reloads from a foreign card. Check your phone model before you rely on it.
- Convenience stores refund small left-over balances when you leave. Refunds above KRW 50,000 need the T-money Town office near Seoul Station.

## ATMs: use the "Global ATM" machines

- Use **Hana, Shinhan or KB** machines marked *Global ATM* or *overseas cards*. Ordinary domestic machines often reject foreign cards.
- Expect an operator fee of **KRW 3,000–4,000**, plus what your own bank charges.
- When the screen asks, **choose KRW**, not your home currency. The conversion offered at the machine costs more.

## QR wallets

- Alipay, WeChat Pay and UnionPay QR users can pay at participating **ZeroPay** merchants. Look for the wallet logos at the counter.
- Since September 2025, Japanese **PayPay** users can pay in Korea too. Finish the identity check in Japan before you travel.

## Tipping

Tipping is not expected in restaurants, hotels or taxis. The price on the menu is the price you pay.

## Your first-day checklist

1. Bring one Visa or Mastercard and one backup card.
2. Withdraw KRW 100,000 at a Global ATM at the airport or the first big station.
3. Buy a T-money card at a convenience store and top it up with KRW 10,000–20,000.
4. Save the [How to pay in Korea](/help/korea/pay) page on your phone for the details.
